01. Brand story

A tool, not a pitch.

Ceres is a facility management SaaS for property managers, engineers, and residents. The kind of product people keep open all day. Our job is to disappear into the work, then surface clarity at exactly the moment it is needed.

Mission

M

Make complex property operations legible at a glance, and trustworthy under scrutiny. Ceres replaces scattered spreadsheets and siloed apps with one room you can actually live in.

Vision

V

Every property team deserves the kind of cool, focused, data-dense surface that engineers take for granted. We are building it for facilities.

Audience

A

Property managers, facilities engineers, resident teams, and the admins who sit between them. People who measure success in resolved tickets, not seats sold.

Brand pillars

01 — Calm

Long-session comfort. No dopamine loops, no celebratory overlays. The product earns trust by being predictable.

02 — Precise

Tabular numerals, exact values, sentence-case labels. Every pixel is in service of legibility under pressure.

03 — Technical

Built by people who have been on-call. The interface respects the user's expertise without assuming it.

04 — Quietly warm

Cold tools fail their humans at 3am. The teal warmth in our brand says: we are with you in this.

The promise, in one sentence.

Built for the people
keeping the lights on.

02. Voice & tone

How Ceres sounds.

Precise, calm, low-theatrics. We use British spellings in product chrome, sentence case for labels and buttons, and full stops in descriptions. We never use exclamation marks or emoji in product surfaces.

Conventions at a glance

Person
Neutral or third-person in docs. Second-person in empty states and helper text. "You can adjust these in Settings."
Casing
Sentence case for labels, headings, buttons. Uppercase only for eyebrow / metadata. "Save changes", not "Save Changes".
Spelling
British in chrome, navigation, marketing. Colours · behaviour · catalogue.
Numbers
Tabular figures everywhere. Abbreviate at scale in cards (1.2k); show exact values in tables.
Verbs
Direct, actionable, single word where possible. Save · Export · Archive · Retry. Avoid Please, Kindly, and the exclamation mark.
Emoji
Never in product chrome. The one exception is user-generated content (chat, comments).

Do / don't

Do
"Ticket updated. Assigned to Maintenance."
Direct, sentence case, no filler.
Don't
"Great news! Your Ticket Has Been Successfully Updated!"
Title case, exclamation, hollow praise.
Do
"No tickets assigned. Create one to get started."
Empty state: factual, with a prompt.
Don't
"Looks like you're all caught up! Nothing here yet."
Filler, informal, adds no information.
03. Logo system

The mark and the word.

The Cerebrum icon and the Ceres wordmark. Use them separately or as a lockup. Never recreate, recolour outside the approved palette, or place on conflicting backgrounds.

Icon

Use the icon alone on small surfaces: app icons, favicons, avatar slots. Never below 20px.

Wordmark

Use where there is room to breathe. Minimum width 80px. Preferred in teal on dark backgrounds.

Lockup

Icon + wordmark together for top-of-page branding, marketing headers, and printed materials.

!
Clear space rule: maintain padding equal to the height of the letter "C" in the wordmark on all sides. Never overlap the mark with other logos or imagery.

06. Iconography

Lucide. Stroke only.

All icons are SVG, stroke-only (no fill), stroke-width: 1.5 default (2 for chevrons and arrows), stroke-linecap: round, stroke-linejoin: round. Standard sizes: 9–13px in product, 16–20px in marketing.

Size
9–11px for sidebar metadata. 12px for nav items. 13px for topbar actions. 16–20px for marketing.
Colour
Default: --text-muted. Active / hover: --text-primary. Accent: --teal-main.
Stroke
stroke-width: 1.5 for all standard icons. 2 for chevrons, arrows, and navigation indicators.
07. Imagery

Cool, cyan, dark.

Photography follows the same restraint as the UI. Scenes should feel like they were shot in a data centre, a machine room, or a building at dusk. Grade: deep shadows, cyan highlights, low saturation.

Server roomCool · Cyan
Building exteriorDusk · Muted
Control roomDark · Focused
Grade
Deep shadows lifted slightly. Cyan midtones. Highlights rolled off. Never warm-toned; never oversaturated.
Subject
Infrastructure, buildings, control surfaces, people at work. Prefer candid, technical, environmental over stock-photo smiling.
Overlay
When placing copy over imagery, use a dark gradient (rgba(0,0,0,0.6)). Never light overlays.

13. Don'ts

What not to do.

The mark and typeface are specific. Any deviation breaks the trust signal the brand has built.

Don't recolour the mark outside the approved palette
Don't rotate or distort the icon
Don't place the teal wordmark on white
Don't stretch or compress the wordmark
Ceres
Don't set the name in an unapproved typeface
Don't use the icon at critically low opacity
