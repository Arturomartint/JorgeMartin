Accentuate

What Building a Language App in the Age of AI Actually Taught Me

Most language apps follow the same loop: input, AI response, gamified reward. It works for a few weeks. The streak keeps you coming back. But the feedback is generic, the corrections feel automated, and somewhere around week six you realise you're completing exercises without actually improving.

The fundamental flaw is that AI alone can't give you what a good tutor can: contextual, personalised, trust-based feedback delivered at the right moment. That gap is what Accentuate is built around.

The Concept

Accentuate is a SaaS platform for language learning built on a single premise: the best learning experience isn't AI or human — it's both, working together. AI handles the heavy lifting: real-time grammar analysis, error detection, and pattern recognition across sessions, powered by Qwen-Max LLM and served via Alibaba Cloud's global CDN. But human tutors sit inside the feedback layer, reviewing, refining, and adding context that the model alone can't generate.

This creates a product category that doesn't really exist yet — faster than booking a tutor, deeper than any app.

The Design Challenges

Three problems dominated the design process.

Transparency without noise. Users needed to understand at a glance when they were receiving AI feedback versus human feedback. Getting that distinction wrong would erode trust in both. The solution was a clear signal layer — visual indicators that communicate the feedback source without cluttering the interface.

Progress that feels real. Language progress is non-linear and genuinely difficult to measure. A streak doesn't mean you're improving. Designing a stats system that reflects genuine skill growth — not just activity — required deciding which metrics actually matter to a learner trying to hold a conversation in six months.

A dashboard that serves two users. Learners and tutors both live inside the same product but need entirely different views of it. Building both surfaces required a clear information architecture that kept the roles distinct without fragmenting the experience.

Key Features

The platform shipped with eight core features across three layers: the learning surface (live practice sessions, tailored assessments, progress tracking), the feedback layer (AI analysis plus human-proofed corrections), and the infrastructure (mobile-responsive, Alibaba Cloud CDN, SEO-optimised content).

The dashboard is a grid-style board that surfaces all active and past sessions at a glance — the kind of view that makes returning users feel oriented instantly, which is the hardest thing to get right in a learning product.

What I Learned

AI products are interface design problems as much as they are engineering problems. The model is table stakes. The design is what determines whether users trust it, return to it, and genuinely improve because of it.

Accentuate pushed me into territory I hadn't worked in before: designing around machine-generated content, handling ambiguous feedback loops, and treating trust as a first-class design dimension rather than an afterthought. The challenge of making a user feel confident that the feedback they just received is worth acting on turned out to be one of the most interesting UX problems I've encountered.

The platform is live at accentuate-five.vercel.app. There's more to build.
